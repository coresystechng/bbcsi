My Journey of Hope and Survival — By Mrs. Grace Ateta

Published: 13 October 2025
Author: Mrs. Grace Ateta

My name is Mrs. Grace Ateata from Benue State, and I proudly call myself not just a survivor, but a warrior and champion of the Benue Breast Cancer Support Initiative (BBCSI). My story began in 2018, when I first noticed a small lump and an uneasy feeling around my right breast. After several visits to the hospital, multiple tests, scans, and biopsies, I finally received the devastating news — it was breast cancer. Fear and uncertainty filled my heart, but I was soon introduced to members of BBCSI, including Mrs. Mnena Lan and Mrs. Hannah Tiohn, whose strength and encouragement gave me the courage to face my journey head-on.

Through BBCSI, I found a strong network of women who understood what I was going through. They provided not only emotional support but also practical guidance — educating me about the treatment process and connecting me to the right medical professionals. With their help, I began treatment at the teaching hospital in Zaria. The organization ensured I wasn’t alone; they checked in on me, encouraged me through my six cycles of chemotherapy, and even assisted me during my mastectomy surgery. Their love and support gave me hope at every stage of recovery.

Today, I am healthy, strong, and grateful. My experience with BBCSI has taught me invaluable lessons about healthy living, self-care, and the power of awareness. I realized that many of the lifestyle choices I once made out of ignorance were harmful, and now I am committed to helping others live better. I have become a breast cancer advocate, sharing my story and teaching other women that cancer is not a death sentence — with early detection, treatment, and the right support, survival is possible.

I am deeply thankful to Professor Stevina Evuleocha-Ugbah, the founder of BBCSI, and to all the amazing women who continue to support and empower survivors like me. The organization not only gave me strength but also restored my confidence and dignity through their care, education, and empowerment programs. Today, I walk tall — a living testimony that early detection saves lives, and that with faith, support, and courage, healing is possible.
