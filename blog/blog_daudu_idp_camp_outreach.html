BBCSI Takes Breast Cancer Awareness Campaign to IDP Camp in Daudu, Benue State

Published: 16 October 2026 · Daudu, Benue State · BBCSI Communications Team

The Benue Breast Cancer Support Initiative (BBCSI), led by its Founder, Prof. Stevina Evuleocha-Ugbah, has taken its breast cancer awareness campaign to an Internally Displaced Persons (IDP) camp in Daudu, Benue State, as part of its commitment to promoting early detection, timely treatment, and access to essential breast health information among vulnerable populations.

Early Detection Saves Lives

During the outreach, Prof. Stevina Evuleocha-Ugbah emphasised the importance of early detection and prompt medical attention in improving breast cancer treatment outcomes and survival rates. She encouraged the women at the camp to prioritise their health and seek professional medical evaluation whenever they notice unusual changes in their breasts.

She reassured the women that breast cancer is not necessarily a death sentence, stressing that early diagnosis and appropriate treatment can significantly improve the chances of survival.

She further encouraged the women to practise breast self-awareness by paying attention to changes in their breasts, including during bathing. She advised them to report any of the following to a qualified health professional without delay:

  • Unusual lumps
  • Swelling
  • Changes in breast size or shape
  • Skin changes
  • Abnormal nipple discharge

She also urged the beneficiaries to become ambassadors of breast cancer awareness by sharing the knowledge acquired during the campaign with their families, friends, and neighbours. According to her, spreading accurate information at the grassroots level is essential to promoting early detection and encouraging women to seek timely medical care.

Voices from the Camp and the Ministry

In their remarks, representatives of the State Emergency Management Agency (SEMA) and the camp coordinator, Mr. Numbe, expressed appreciation to Prof. Stevina Evuleocha-Ugbah and the BBCSI team for bringing the awareness campaign to the displaced women.

They commended the initiative and encouraged the beneficiaries to share the information with other residents of the camp and members of their respective communities to promote greater awareness of breast cancer, early detection, and the importance of seeking appropriate medical care.

Also speaking at the event, Mrs. Ruth Ningi, Head of the Out-of-School Children Unit, Department of Child Development, who represented the Honourable Commissioner for Women Affairs and Social Welfare, Mrs. Theresa Odachi Ikwue, delivered a message of encouragement on behalf of the Ministry.

She acknowledged the importance of initiatives that promote the health, welfare, and well-being of vulnerable women and encouraged the beneficiaries to take the information provided seriously and share it with others.

Humanitarian Support

As part of the humanitarian outreach, the Benue Breast Cancer Support Initiative also presented relief materials to the IDP camp to help address some of the basic needs of the displaced persons. The gesture demonstrated the organisation’s commitment to combining health advocacy with humanitarian support for vulnerable communities.

Reaching Beyond Conventional Settings

The outreach reflects BBCSI’s determination to take breast cancer education beyond conventional settings and reach women who may face barriers to accessing reliable health information and medical services.

Through sustained advocacy, community engagement, and humanitarian interventions, Prof. Stevina Evuleocha-Ugbah and the Benue Breast Cancer Support Initiative continue to advance breast health awareness, encourage early detection, and empower women to take proactive steps towards protecting their health and well-being.
