A Complete Guide to Breast Self-Examination (BSE)

Published: 25 October 2025 · Author: BBCSI

Understanding Breast Cancer

Breast cancer is a disease that occurs when cells in the breast grow abnormally and form a lump or mass called a tumor. These cancerous cells can invade nearby tissues or spread to other parts of the body if not detected early.

Early detection saves lives! Regular breast self-examination is a crucial step in identifying potential signs of breast cancer at an early stage.

Step-by-Step Guide to Breast Self-Examination
Look

Stand in front of a mirror with your shoulders straight and your hands on your hips. Look for any changes in breast shape, size, or color such as dimpling, swelling, redness, or changes in the nipple.

Raise Arms

Raise your arms and look again for the same changes. This position helps reveal any irregularities that might not be visible when your arms are down.

Check for Discharge

Gently squeeze each nipple to see if any fluid (watery, milky, or bloody) comes out. Any unusual discharge should be reported to your healthcare provider.

Feel While Lying Down

Lie down and use your right hand to feel your left breast, and your left hand for your right breast. Use the pads of your fingers in a circular motion, covering the entire breast from:

  • Top to bottom
  • Side to side
  • Collarbone to the top of your abdomen
  • Armpit to your cleavage
Feel While Standing or Sitting

Many women find it easiest to do this step in the shower. Move your hand over the entire breast area using the same circular motions as in step 4.

Important Tip

Do your self-check once a month, about a week after your period ends (or on a fixed day each month if you no longer menstruate). Consistency is key in detecting any changes in your breast tissue.
